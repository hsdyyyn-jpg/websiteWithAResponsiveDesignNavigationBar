pestalotiopsis microspora

It was first discovered in 1880 by a mycologist on the fallen foliage of common ivy in Buenos Aries, Argentina.

This fungus has the ability to break down and consume polyurethane plastic, which is a material used in foam, protective coating, and synthetic fabrics. Pestalotiopsis microspora is important because it is the first fungal species found by people which have the ability to sustain itself by eating polyurethane in an oxygen-free environment.

Pleurotus ostreatus

The Pleurotus ostreatus is a saprotroph which feeds on and decomposes dead wood. In doing so, it returns vital elements and minerals to the soil, making them available for other plants and organisms. These mechanisms benefit the forest ecosystem.

Pleurotus ostreatus could be used to clean up environmental pollution, and they are successfully used to clean up diesel oil which contaminated the soil. Also, they are capable of growing on and breaking down plastic bags and are helpful for the degradation of renewable polyethylene.
